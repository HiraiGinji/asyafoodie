TOYO EATERY

Toyo Eatery in Manila, helmed by chef Jordy Navarra, brings everyday Filipino ingredients to the fine dining stage. Known for inventive dishes like the “Bahay Kubo” salad—featuring 18 native vegetables—and a 12-hour, three-cut pork barbecue, the restaurant celebrates Filipino culture, heritage, and flavors. Its name, meaning soy sauce in Tagalog, reflects the complexity behind seemingly simple elements.

Beyond food, Toyo crafts a full cultural experience with menus ranging from à la carte to six-course tastings, all served in a proudly Filipino-designed space. Their cocktails feature local spirits like tapuey, transformed into unique drinks like the Tapuey Sour. With a nearby bakery, Panaderya Toyo, Navarra and team continue their mission to represent who they are through food—constantly learning, evolving, and honoring their roots.

Mämmi

Served in a rustic, earthy bowl that complements its dark color, the mämmi takes center stage. Its deep, almost black hue hints at the long hours it's been baked with rye malt and rye flour. The surface appears slightly textured, perhaps with a subtle sheen.

Yakitori

Tender chicken thighs, glistening with a subtly sweet and savory glaze, charred just enough to impart a smoky depth. Interspersed are plump, juicy chicken meatballs, their surface caramelized to a beautiful mahogany. Adding a refreshing counterpoint are crisp lengths of green onion, their mild sharpness cutting through the richness of the chicken.

Mochi

Served in a warm, earthy-toned bowl that cradles its gentle form, the mochi possesses a smooth, almost ethereal quality. Its subtle sheen catches the light, inviting you to experience its unique springiness.
